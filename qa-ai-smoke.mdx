---
title: Organizing Your Documentation
---

A well-structured documentation site helps readers find what they need without
hunting through unrelated pages. Group related topics together, lead each page
with the question it answers, and keep navigation shallow so the most important
guides are never more than a click or two away.

## Start With the Reader's Goal

Every page should open by stating what the reader will be able to do once they
finish it. Front-load the outcome, then fill in the steps. Readers skim before
they commit, so the first paragraph has to earn the rest of their attention.

## Keep Navigation Shallow

Deep nesting hides content. Prefer a handful of clearly named top-level groups
over a sprawling tree. When a group grows past seven or eight pages, that is
usually a sign it should be split along a sharper boundary.

## Revisit Structure as You Grow

The structure that fit ten pages rarely fits fifty. Schedule a periodic review
of your navigation, prune pages that no longer earn their place, and merge thin
pages that would serve readers better as a single comprehensive guide.
